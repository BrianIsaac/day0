# The manager's answers

Kestrel Supply Co. is a synthetic company built for the Day0 demonstration; these answers are
invented.

What the manager types in each employee's Day-1 one-to-one, and the note the manager gives the
logistics desk when it stops. Two rules from the 14 September runs hold every line here: describe
the work in the tickets' own words, and name each system the way the handbook names it. A charter
is built from what the manager typed, and an adjective no ticket can prove becomes a condition the
plan then has to meet.

The one-to-one asks about seven topics in this order: why the hire, the role and its goals, who
the employee works with, what to read, the tools, what to do first, and what is still open. Answer
the question the employee actually asked; the answers below are in topic order. If it asks
something else, answer "Nope, that's it." at most three times.

## Deploy

Three employees, names only, no role, no skills and no scope. The names are placeholders until the
operator renames them: Priya for revenue operations, Mateo for finance close, Aiko for the
logistics desk.

## Revenue operations

1. Honestly, Q3 close is a mess. Stuff falls through the cracks between sales and finance,
   follow-ups depend on whoever remembers, and nobody wants another meeting about it. I need
   someone keeping the tickets in Linear moving and keeping an audit trail.
2. You're our revops coordinator for the Q3 close. Triage what comes in, work the tickets in
   Linear, keep the audit notes on them, draft updates for me, and flag anything that smells like
   risk. First month, learn how we work and get your access sorted. Month two, you run the routine
   tickets yourself.
3. There's the Linear admin for access and workflow, the Slack admin for channels, and business
   systems for the CRM. Go through me for all of them for now, I'll intro you.
4. The onboarding page, then the revenue operations handbook and its runbooks.
5. Linear for the real work, team REVOPS, project Q3 close: the audit note, the Looker pipeline
   tile refresh and the Northstar reconcile are all tickets in Linear. Asks come in on Slack in
   #revops-asks and in #ops-requests, the shared request channel; #revops is the team channel. The
   pipeline numbers live on the Looker pipeline tile, web only. Northstar CRM has the accounts but
   we've got no approved way in yet.
6. Nothing specific. Have a look at the queue, figure out what is stuck on access, bring me a
   draft. Don't send anything out without me.
7. Not sure we'll get you into Northstar CRM before close. And I haven't decided when to let you
   post without asking.

## Logistics desk

   recording each exception and drafting the customer notice as soon as the ticket comes in.
2. You're on the logistics desk for shipment exceptions. Work the exception tickets in Linear,

   from the handbook's templates. First month, learn the process and get your access sorted;
   after that, the routine exceptions are yours.

4. The onboarding page, then the logistics desk handbook and the exception runbook.
5. Linear, team LOG, project Shipment exceptions. Slack: #logistics-desk is the desk's channel and
   #ops-requests is the shared request channel.
6. Pick up the open exception tickets. Don't send anything out without me.
7. Nothing else yet. I haven't decided when to let you post without asking.

## The logistics correction

When the SH-4471 exception stops and asks which notice to use, the manager retries it with this
note, word for word:

> For a Meridian Freight hold at Port Klang with no revised ETA, use the "Delay, ETA unconfirmed,
> next update by <time>" template, with the next update by 17:00 SGT the next business day.

The supervised fallback, only if two rehearsals in a row do not stop: the manager rejects the held
notice with the same text as the reason, which is kept as a correction all the same.
